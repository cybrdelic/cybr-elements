# 02 material-motion repair

The user rejected the preceding water, earth and lightning films; water was
the worst. Fire remains explicitly approved. Air is outside this repair.
Technical delivery from the preceding pass is not evidence of acceptance.

## What was wrong

- Water was supplied as a nearly stationary, shallow filled logo volume. A
  FLIP solver and a water IOR did not make that source behave like a moving jet.
- Earth used damped springs toward individual letter positions and spherical
  contact proxies. Its motion was typography assembly, and the stones read as
  uniform gravel.
- Lightning used a permanent conductor graph and weak standing current. It
  lacked the formation/discharge/restrike rhythm and branch hierarchy of the
  earlier point-charge growth renderer.

## Candidate changes

Water uses the original CPU APIC/FLIP pressure/capillary solver. Its emitter
now supplies disks normal to curved 3D streams. Radius varies with the local
width of the approved02 artwork. The source has depth travel, axial momentum
and mild cross-section rotation. Existing water is never attracted back to
the lettering. Supply ends near2seconds; free transport continues before
gravity ramps up. Unresolved solitary spray parcels use persistent,
volume-normalized truncated-Pareto fragments; dense drops remain coherent.
That fragmentation is a subgrid graphics closure, not resolved atomization.
The optical world transmits the actual black backdrop while retaining studio
reflection lighting. No image opacity fade is used.

Earth reuses Blender Bullet convex-hull contacts and the original layered
stone assets.540bodies have inherited source momentum, broad sizes, depth
variation, rotation and collisions. There are no per-stone target springs.
The source has68large clasts, with secondary grains following gravity and drag.
Shared rock meshes receive real geometric relief and larger mineral color
variation. The collision hulls approximate the fine render-surface relief.

The procedural surface pilot was superseded by CC0 scans for the larger
stones: Poly Haven's `boulder_01` by Rico Cilliers, and `rock_07` / `rock_09`
by Jenelle van Heerden. Original UV diffuse/normal/roughness maps are retained.
Downloads are about26MB total; hashes match the provider's metadata.
Exact sources and attribution are in `scans/attribution.json`. The smallest
chips retain the original layered meshes. Bullet hulls remain coarse proxies.

Lightning returns to the earlier3D point-charge growth approximation.
Variable-width regions around the artwork's principal strokes guide growth;
the30channels can fork outside those regions. Short leader/return-stroke
trains replace standing current. Secondary aerosol is transported by the
existing pressure-projected grid and lit by excited channels. This is a
graphics electrical model with authored bending guidance, not plasma science.

Five independently grown channel families are used across the five discharge
trains. Millisecond return strokes keep their established channel; later
discharges get different geometry. This replaces the fixed blinking network.

## Review record

1. Water's first CPU pilot was rejected locally: more movement, still a thin
   glass-like sheet. Preserved in `water-pilot-sheet-rejected/`.
2. A rounded extrusion still produced planar, oversized fragments and excessive
   bright interior reflection. Rejected in `water-pilot-extrusion-rejected/`.
3. Normal-plane jets produced independent rounded streams and evolving
   topology. Selected for full sequence review, with a finer spray closure.
4. Earth motion was restored first; the CPU close-up exposed overly clean
   clast surfaces. Relief/material refinement followed before full rendering.
5. Lightning's first narrow-corridor growth pilot had too few forks. Wider
   field guidance and longer growth intervals restored branch hierarchy.
6. Independent later-discharge networks replace the remaining fixed-pattern
   repetition. The third lightning pilot records the changing channels.

## Resource and continuity recovery

The queue stopped at its disk-headroom guard. Heavy phases were serialized.
Four existing `.pt` checkpoints were compressed transparently with NTFS LZX;
442,584,092bytes were freed, with all four SHA256 hashes unchanged. No videos,
source code or checkpoint contents were deleted.

Completed water JPEGs are retained during recovery. The deterministic solver
and only the classifier/spray state are replayed; completed surfaces and
images are not rendered again. The late reconstruction bound was corrected
for the wider camera. It now lies below the visible frame; stable original
particle IDs are preserved through that cull so spray histories are not
assigned to different particles. Earth can render during CPU history replay.

CPU pilots are1280×720; candidate films are1920×1080/30fps. Full videos must be
decoded and visually inspected, including release and black intervals, before
the player changes. Metrics establish integrity, not quality or user approval.

The final player can retain old media and offer an immediate previous/revision
comparison. Approved fire and air hashes must remain identical.

## Release boundary correction

The first full water encode exposed a hidden basin-floor rebound at the bottom
of the frame. It is retained as water-basin-candidate.mp4. The final selected
water run uses a mass-accounted outflow at solverY=.12 (worldZ=-2.457), below
the camera. Primary particles crossing that plane leave the domain before
contacting its floor. Stable source IDs preserve spray history through particle
compaction. Existing detached fragments continue under gravity and drag until
they also leave the image; there is no global fade or in-frame deletion plane.
Frames0..135 are preserved byte-for-byte; only the release is rerendered.
The final water sequence has240frames/8seconds.

The full release review also exposed sparse primary parcels stalling after
the bulk fluid left. This occurred once fewer than2048primary markers remained.
The selected final film preserves frames0..175 and continues those unresolved
residual positions and velocities under gravity and quadratic sphere drag,
with outflow below the frame. The first empty state is frame222; the ending
contains18empty frames. This is a hybrid liquid/sparse-parcel treatment, not
a claim that the fluid grid resolves subgrid droplet dynamics. The earlier
full release remains available in water-release-frames/; final selected
frames are water-final-frames/. The transition and encoded ending must be
visually reviewed before publication.
